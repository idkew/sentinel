1. Who is responsible for your data

There are two roles, and which one applies depends on the data:

  • We are the controller of staff account data (section 2, "Staff"), of the feedback you send us, and of the technical and security data needed to run the Service. We decide why and how that data is used.
  • The operator (the owner and team of an experience) is the controller of player data in its workspace, and we are its processor (or "service provider"). The operator decides that moderation happens, who the moderators are, what rules apply and how long records are kept within the limits below. We process player data only to provide the Service to that operator and follow their instructions as built into the Service. Each operator's data is kept separate from every other operator's.

Operators are responsible for their own legal duties to their players, including telling players that moderation tools are used and getting any consent the law requires. A player who wants to use their rights can contact us or the operator of the experience. If you contact us about player data we will pass the request on to the operator where needed, or deal with it directly where we can.

3. How we use information

  • To provide the Service: show operators their players and records, carry out bans, mutes and kicks the operator chooses, and deliver notices.
  • To keep the Service secure and fair: sign-in checks, two-step verification, rate limits, and detecting misuse.
  • To take backups and to restore a workspace if something goes wrong.
  • To answer you, including support and feedback.
  • To meet legal obligations and to protect people, our rights and the Service.

We do not use player data to build marketing profiles, to advertise to players, or to train AI models. No decision with legal or similarly significant effect is made about anyone solely by automated means. Staff always make the moderation decision.

8. Security

We take reasonable steps to protect personal information:

  • We never see your Google or Roblox password.
  • Sessions use signed, HttpOnly, secure cookies. Traffic is encrypted in transit.
  • Access is limited by role, and each game's data is kept separate from every other game's, with its own secret key that can be replaced at any time.
  • Secrets such as authenticator keys and Roblox API keys are stored encrypted. Recovery codes are stored hashed.
  • Staff invites expire, are single-use and can be locked to one Roblox account. Staff and owners can turn on two-step verification, and owners can require it for administrators.
  • Requests are rate-limited, and every moderation action is recorded in an audit log.
  • Support access by the people who run the Service. To help an operator or investigate a problem, the people who run the Service (the site operators) can open a workspace read-only and see what its owner would see, including chat logs and moderation records. Nothing can be changed in this view, it ends by itself after 15 minutes, and every visit is written to that workspace's own audit log, which the owner can see. Site operators also keep a private log of their own actions.
  • A copy of each workspace is backed up every night.

No system is perfectly secure, so we cannot guarantee absolute security. If we become aware of a breach that is likely to harm people, we will notify the affected operators and, where the law requires, the authorities and affected people, without undue delay. Please tell us promptly if you think something is wrong, at the address in section 13.

10. Your rights and how to use them

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to: know what we hold about you; get a copy; correct it; delete it; restrict or object to how we use it; move it to another service; withdraw consent you gave; and complain to your data protection authority. People in California, and some other US states, may also have the right to know, delete and correct personal information and to opt out of its sale or sharing. We do not sell or share personal information in those senses, so there is nothing to opt out of.

  • Staff: use the tools in the dashboard where they exist (for example to turn off two-step verification or unlink Roblox or Discord), or contact us to delete your account. If you are in a workspace, its owner can remove you.
  • Players: contact us or the operator of the experience. We can export and erase a player's stored data in a workspace on request, or across every workspace that uses the Service. Requests are handled by the site operators, and each one (export, check or deletion) is logged with when it was made, who asked for it, and what was done. Before anything is deleted we first check what is stored, and a deletion has to be confirmed separately.
  • How: email {{email}} with your Roblox username or ID and the name of the experience, if you know it. We may ask you to confirm you are who you say you are, for example by showing control of the account, so that we do not give your data to someone else. For a player who is a child, a parent or guardian can make the request.
  • Time: we aim to answer within 30 days. If we refuse a request we will tell you why and how to complain. In the US you may also appeal our decision by replying to us.

Some records may need to stay where the law requires it, or where deleting them would stop an operator keeping a ban in force against someone who broke the rules. We will explain this if it applies.
